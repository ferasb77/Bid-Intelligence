# York P27-070 Decision-Brief Benchmark

## A. Product Purpose

### 1. Authoritative Product Mission
The primary purpose of Bid Intelligence UNDERSTAND is:
> **"Provide an evidence-based decision brief to the bid decision maker."**

The primary report is **NOT**:
- A procurement summary or metadata sheet
- A compliance checklist
- A legal contract digest
- An internal system or pipeline audit
- A dump of canonical database objects
- A dump of raw specialist findings or execution traces

### 2. Core Decision-Maker Questions
Every page and section of the primary brief must directly help an executive or bid lead answer:
1. **What is the buyer actually buying?** (Substantive operational scope and business intent beyond commodity labels).
2. **Why is this opportunity relevant / strategically meaningful?** (Buyer mandate, institutional priorities, funding or program drivers).
3. **What kind of provider is the buyer looking for?** (Organizational profile, delivery posture, team depth).
4. **What technical capabilities and credentials matter?** (Non-negotiable skills, subject matter authority, methodologies).
5. **How will the proposal be judged?** (True mathematical scoring structure, evaluation stages, evaluator priorities).
6. **What must we prove with evidence?** (Proof burden, mandatory RFP evidence items vs response strategy recommendations).
7. **What could materially weaken or block a bid?** (Disqualifying gates, restrictive commercial clauses, capacity choke-points).
8. **What should the bid team resolve before writing?** (Go/no-go discriminators, scoping tradeoffs, clarification questions).

### 3. Length and Executive Design Contract
- **Target Length**: 6–10 pages total (preferred 7–8 pages).
- **Format**: Single stand-alone primary **Bid Intelligence Brief**.
- **Optional Supporting Asset**: Detailed Evidence Appendix / Source Detail (separate document containing comprehensive requirement registers, full legal clause catalogs, and specialist extraction traces).
- **Executive Rule**: Maximum decision density per page. A busy bid executive must be able to absorb the complete strategic context, risks, and proof burdens within 5–10 minutes without wading through raw database dumps or administrative boilerplate.

### 2. Multi-Stage Evaluation Rules
- **Written vs Shortlist Stages** (*RFP FACT*): The initial written proposal is scored out of 100.0 points. Part 4 (Short List Presentation) is a separate, conditional stage evaluated for an additional 20.0 points "as required" by York University.
- **Minimum Technical Threshold** (*UNKNOWN / NONE STATED*): No numerical minimum threshold (e.g., 75% or 60/80 points) is stated in the buyer-issued documents reviewed. The product must not invent an unstated threshold.
- **Tie-Breaking Sequence** (*RFP FACT - Page 18*):
  In the event of an identical proposal score, ties are resolved strictly by:
  1. The Proposal with the highest points for Price; and if still tied,
  2. The Proposal with the highest points for ability to deliver services; and if still tied,
  3. A coin toss.
- **Evaluation Discipline** (*RFP FACT - Page 26*): York explicitly cautions proponents that proposals will be evaluated solely upon the disclosed evaluation criteria.

## K. Evidence & Epistemic Rules

Every statement in a Bid Intelligence report must strictly belong to exactly one of the following five classes:

```
[ EPISTEMIC CLASS ]                  [ DEFINITION & SOURCE OF TRUTH ]
1. RFP FACT                     -->  Fact extracted verbatim or directly normalized from official
                                     buyer-issued RFP documents, schedules, or addenda.
2. EXTERNAL BUYER FACT          -->  Independently verified institutional data from official buyer
                                     domains (.ca, .edu, public directives) with recorded source metadata.
3. BID INTELLIGENCE             -->  Analytical inference connecting RFP facts to operational risks,
   INTERPRETATION                    evaluation weight implications, or delivery feasibility.
4. BID STRATEGY / PROOF         -->  Advisory recommendation suggesting proof artifacts or response
   RECOMMENDATION                    approaches to strengthen scoring alignment.
5. UNKNOWN                      -->  Information explicitly not established in the reviewed documents;
                                     must be declared as unknown rather than invented.
```

### Prohibitions:
- **No Hallucinated Requirements**: Never invent a buyer requirement or convert an extraction failure into a buyer omission.
- **No Predictive Evaluator Guarantees**: Never use predictive phrasing like "evaluators will penalize", "massive scoring edge", or "disqualifies solo practitioners" unless explicitly stated in buyer rules. Use bounded interpretations (e.g., "the RFP's explicit requirement indicates a preference for...").
- **No Brand Invention**: Never insert specific commercial technology brands (e.g., Claude, ChatGPT, Apollo, Clay) unless explicitly named by the buyer.
- **No Leaked Internal IDs**: Internal database keys, specialist prefixes (`REQ-13`, `SPECIALIST_FULL:4`), or pipeline execution tokens must never appear in customer prose.

## L. Report Quality Gates

A generated Bid Intelligence Brief will be graded as **FAIL** if any of the following quality defects occur:
1. **Omission of Verified RFP Capabilities**: Fails to surface Canadian SME ecosystem knowledge, deep bench resilience, sales playbooks, AI integration, existing framework sample, or detailed methodology.
2. **Predictive Evaluator Language**: Uses unsupported predictive claims ("evaluators will reject/penalize") instead of bounded interpretations.
3. **Invented Technology Brands**: Injects unmentioned third-party vendor or tool brands into buyer requirements or recommendations.
4. **Masquerading Proof Recommendations**: Presents recommended response strategies as mandatory buyer requirements.
5. **Phantom External Buyer Facts**: Asserts external institutional facts without recorded source URL and title metadata.
6. **Excusing Extraction Failures as Buyer Omissions**: Generates text stating "the buyer did not specify" when the omission is actually a tool extraction defect.
7. **Priority Inversion**: Allocates more visual space or priority to standard administrative clauses than to substantive technical capability requirements.
8. **Duplicate Evaluation Criteria**: Generates repeated criteria rows or fragments criteria into conflicting tables.
9. **Leaked Pipeline Artifacts**: Exposes internal debug keys, specialist IDs, or database entity names in narrative text.
